## Cambridge origin and authorship

The Cambridge Quarters are associated with the chimes first installed at
Great St Mary's, Cambridge, in 1793. Great St Mary's distinguishes the
Cambridge name from the later popular name “Westminster Chimes.” Grove's
account repeats a traditional attribution involving Dr Jowett, Dr Crotch and
the fifth bar of Handel's “I know that my Redeemer liveth.” That attribution
should be presented as historical testimony, not as an independently proven
composer credit: Starmer notes that the detailed history depends on testimony
preserved through Mr Amps and Dr Raven.

Sources: [Great St Mary's — The Bells](https://www.greatstmarys.org/bells),
[Grove-derived Cambridge Quarters article](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/A_Dictionary_of_Music_and_Musicians/Cambridge_Quarters),
[Starmer 1907, pp. 8–9](https://whitingsociety.org.uk/old-ringing-books/starmer-chimes-and-chime-tunes-file-01.pdf).

## Westminster adoption and quarter-hour function

Starmer records that the Cambridge chimes were copied for the Houses of
Parliament in 1859–60. The Elizabeth Tower installation is a scoped historical
example: its quarter bells play the quarter pattern and the separate Great
Bell strikes the hour. This must not be generalized to every commercial clock
that uses a Westminster-labelled variant.

Source: [UK Parliament — The Great Bell and the quarter bells](https://www.parliament.uk/about/living-heritage/building/palace/big-ben/facts-figures/great-bell/)
and [Elizabeth Tower virtual tour](https://virtualtours-assets.parliament.uk/elizabeth-tower/index.html).

## Score editions and differences

The reference edition is the D-major Cambridge/Grove notation witness. It
records Q1–Q4 as 4, 8, 12 and 16 quarter-note events, followed by a D
whole-note hour event. Grove also describes a Royal Exchange sequence change;
Starmer describes the Royal Exchange copy and later Parliament adoption. The
Doncaster arrangement is a different reduced/altered arrangement and must not
be silently merged with the Grove witness.

The accompanying audio is an editorial reference in the Grove notation's
pitch-class order. Its D4 melody, D3 hour, A4=440, equal temperament, 100 BPM
and phrase gaps are performance choices. They are not evidence of Big Ben's
octave, tuning, bell partials or mechanical timing. Parliament's official
pages also contain a G-sharp/G-natural first-quarter discrepancy; this dossier
does not resolve it by inference.

## Clock and relation scope

Clock mechanism descriptions belong to the Elizabeth Tower/Westminster
installation and must remain installation-scoped. A Variant or Movement that
is documented as configured with Westminster music may support a Music
relation, but that does not prove the same music for the Brand, all Models or
all Products. A concrete physical specimen and a commercial listing remain
different scopes. Each relation candidate requires its own registered
predicate, source/evidence path and direct/derived provenance.
